📘 Unit 2: Digital Literacy — Stay Safe Online

2.1 Your Data and Cookies

A cookie is a small file a website puts in your browser to remember you. It is not food and cannot hurt your computer.

  • Essential cookies: Needed for the website to work. You cannot turn these off.
  • Performance cookies: Collect information on how you use the site to improve it.
  • Functionality cookies: Record what you do (like filling a form) to make the page more personal next time.
  • Advertising cookies: Record what you look at to show personal adverts. (Privacy risk).
  • Law: Websites must make it clear what cookies they use.

2.2 Cyber Choices

  • Cybercrime: Criminal activity using a computer/internet.
  • Phishing: Fake website/email looking like a real bank to steal login details.
  • Identity Theft: Stealing personal info (passport no.) to pretend to be you.
  • Hacking: Breaking into a computer system to steal data.
  • Scamming: Emails asking for money, selling fake products, or threatening you.
  • Malware: Software designed to commit crimes (steal, damage, or stop computer).
  • Spyware: Records what you type to steal login details.
  • Ransomware: Encrypts your data and asks for money to unlock it.
  • Trojan: Malware hidden inside another program.
  • Virus: Spreads and infects files, can destroy data.
  • Ethical Hacking: "White hat" hackers legally finding weaknesses to make systems stronger.

2.3 Protect Your Computer

  • Anti-Virus (AV): Detects, blocks, and quarantines malware using signatures.
  • Firewall: Protective barrier monitoring incoming/outgoing traffic.
  • Email Safety: Beware of phishing, use strong passwords, enable 2FA, keep software updated, use safe usernames.

2.4, 2.5 & 2.6 Digital Footprint, Privacy & Cyberbullying

  • Digital Footprint: Trail of data you leave online (posts, searches). Posts are persistent (stay forever).
  • Privacy: Control what you share. Turn off location, disable auto-login, change passwords.
  • Cyberbullying: Using digital tools to harass. Forms: Harassment, Impersonation, Exclusion, Outing/Dissing. Action: Save evidence, do not reply, block, report.

📗 Unit 6: Understanding Technology — Networks

6.1 Network Hardware

  • Server: High-performance computer providing services to clients.
  • Hub vs Switch: Hub sends data to ALL devices (less secure). Switch sends data ONLY to the specific recipient (faster, secure). "A Switch is Smarter".
  • Router: Connects different networks together (e.g., LAN to Internet).
  • Modem: Converts digital data to analog signals for transmission.
  • WAP: Wireless Access Point connects devices to wired network wirelessly.
  • NIC: Network Interface Card (hardware to connect to network).

6.2 How a Network Fits Together

  • Home Hub: Combines router, modem, switch, and WAP.
  • LAN: Local Area Network (school, office).
  • Copper Cable: Electrical pulses, max 100m.
  • Fibre-Optic Cable: Pulses of light, faster, longer distances.
  • Server Room: Needs air conditioning.

6.3, 6.4 & 6.5 Network Communication

  • Protocol: Rules for exchanging data.
  • Data Packets: Files split into chunks. Contains Destination IP, Sender IP, Sequence number, Error-checking data.
  • Packet Switching: Packets take independent routes, arrive out of order, then reassembled using sequence numbers. Missing packets are re-sent.
  • Troubleshooting: Check cables, Wi-Fi toggle, passwords, or power-cycle (reboot) the router.

6.6 In the Cloud

  • Cloud Computing: Remote storage/apps via internet.
  • Advantages: Access anywhere, real-time collaboration, auto-backup.
  • Disadvantages: Needs internet, subscription costs, privacy concerns.
